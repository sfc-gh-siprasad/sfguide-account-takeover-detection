See the whole takeover, not just one odd login.

A login from a new phone is normal. So is a password reset, a new payee, or a larger transfer. A takeover is these steps in a short sequence, often from a device and country the customer has never used. Fraud and cyber teams usually see them in separate tools. This example joins identity, session and transaction events for each customer. It flags six explainable takeover patterns and ranks customers by a tunable score. An investigator gets a timeline and a drafted case summary.

How it works

  1. Synthetic data. 1,500 customers with 60 days of logins, MFA events, account changes and transfers. 40 injected takeovers across four attack styles, plus 71 legitimate look-alikes: travellers, VPN users, new phones and large purchases.
  2. Six rules as SQL views. Takeover sequence (MATCH_RECOGNIZE), impossible travel (HAVERSINE, LAG), new device and country, MFA fatigue, credential stuffing and unusual amount. Each rule records its evidence, and in Snowflake mode the app reads its hits from these views.
  3. Scored alerts. An ATO_ALERTS view adds weights from a RULE_WEIGHTS table, so the score is a sum you can explain.
  4. Cortex AI_COMPLETE. Drafts a case summary from the evidence for the top 20 alerts and stores it, so model use stays small and bounded.
  5. Streamlit app. Runs locally on sample data with no account, or against Snowflake, including as Streamlit in Snowflake.

Try it

  • In your browser: open the hosted example. No account or install. Change rule weights or the threshold and watch alerts and precision change.
  • On your machine: Python 3 with pip; about 5 minutes. Runs the Streamlit app on bundled sample data.
  • Your Snowflake account: about 10 minutes. Needs Cortex AI_COMPLETE with one supported model. Uses one XSMALL warehouse with 60-second auto-suspend; the README gives a cost estimate and a cleanup script.
  • Least privilege: setup creates a dedicated role with only the grants the example needs.

Good to know

  • The rules and thresholds are illustrative, not a recommended detection standard. Tune them to your own data and risk appetite.
  • Precision and recall come from a synthetic data set with known answers. Real rates depend on your customers and attacks.
  • The rules are views, not Dynamic Tables, because Dynamic Tables do not support MATCH_RECOGNIZE. They run when queried.
  • Case summaries are drafts for an investigator. They do not decide a case. Sample-mode summaries are templated, not model output.
